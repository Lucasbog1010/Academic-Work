# Gemstones and Solid-State Lasers

**Academic literature-review project | Physics of Functional Materials (LMAPR2014) | UCLouvain | 2025–2026**

[**Read the full report (PDF)**](https://github.com/Lucasbog1010/Academic-Work/tree/main/Optics_Project/Gemstones_lasers_Report.pdf)

## Project overview

This group report explores how the electronic structure and optical properties of gemstone crystals make them useful as gain media in solid-state lasers. It connects fundamental light–matter interactions with the practical properties, fabrication, and applications of laser-grade materials.

The report addresses three questions:

1. What causes colour in gemstones, and how can it be tuned?
2. How does a laser work?
3. Why are gemstones used in powerful laser systems?

## Topics covered

- **Laser fundamentals:** absorption, spontaneous and stimulated emission, Einstein coefficients, population inversion, optical pumping, and two-, three-, and four-level systems.
- **Quantum and solid-state physics:** electronic energy levels, spin–orbit coupling, crystal-field splitting, and the role of dopant ions in optical absorption and emission.
- **Functional materials:** ruby (Cr-doped corundum), titanium-doped sapphire, and alexandrite; crystal structures, optical and mechanical properties, and colour-change effects.
- **Materials manufacturing:** synthetic crystal production, including the Czochralski and Verneuil methods and the importance of crystal purity and dopant control.
- **Laser technologies:** ruby, Ti:sapphire, and alexandrite lasers, plus market context and future prospects.

## Why this project is relevant to engineering

The work links **materials composition and microstructure** to **functional performance**, and considers how manufacturing processes influence the quality of optical crystals. It is particularly relevant to materials engineering, photonics, advanced manufacturing, and technical research roles.
